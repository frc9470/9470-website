Team 9470 / Ctrl-Alt-Defeat

A student-led Bay Area FRC team open to the wider community.

Ctrl-Alt-Defeat 9470 is based around BASIS Independent Fremont and welcomes students from across the surrounding Bay Area community. We compete in FIRST Robotics Competition, where teams design, build, program, and compete with a new robot every season.

Team 9470 is student-led: mentors and parents support the work, but students are expected to own planning, execution, communication, and results.

The Competition

Every January, the game resets and the clock starts.

For students new to robotics, FRC is simple to understand: each January, teams receive a brand-new game and only a few intense weeks to build a robot that can play it.

That includes strategy, CAD, manufacturing, wiring, software, testing, fundraising, documentation, and competition operations. At events, students drive, repair, scout, and adapt in real time.

On the field, the robot plays short matches with alliance partners.

Off the field, students keep the robot running and improve it between rounds.

Build Season

Team 9470 is a serious commitment, not a drop-in club.

Off-season usually runs weekly Saturday workshop blocks (typically 9:00 AM-4:00 PM, split sessions), with occasional Sunday sessions. Build season often expands to weekday evenings and full weekends as deadlines tighten.

  1. 01

    Train and specialize

    Members join a primary division (mechanical design, fabrication, electrical, software, business, outreach, media, or demo/testing) and build role-specific skills.

  2. 02

    Cross-train across teams

    Students are encouraged to contribute beyond one lane: design supports fabrication, software supports testing, and business supports competition operations.

  3. 03

    Earn competition readiness

    Competition participation is earned through attendance, completed training, safe practices, and reliable contribution under pressure.

  4. 04

    Deliver under deadlines

    Students learn to communicate clearly, ask for help early, solve problems quickly, and follow through on commitments during long competition weekends.

Why It Matters

Why students join: real work, real growth, real teamwork.

Members gain practical experience in engineering, programming, manufacturing, business, media, outreach, and leadership while building something real with real deadlines.

No prior robotics experience is required—what matters most is commitment, curiosity, maturity, and willingness to learn.
